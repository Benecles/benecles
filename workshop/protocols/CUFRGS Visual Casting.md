# CUFRGS Visual Casting

*The master brief for every figure on the site. Written by the CEO, 03/10. It sits above the Figure Library (craft and tests), Visual Genres (forms) and the kit (primitives): those say what a good figure is made of; this says what each lesson's figure must BE.*

## The promise

Every lesson's figure is cast for that lesson the way a film casts an actor for one role. A reader who has seen thirty lessons never feels they've seen a figure before, yet every figure is obviously from the same house: same paper, same inks, same hand, same care. **Uniqueness lives in what is depicted; consistency lives in how it is drawn.** Apple's version: the same materials, finishes and craft across every product, and each product's form born from its one job.

## What never changes (the house)

The reader should be able to tear a figure out of the site, without its caption, and still know where it came from.

- **Paper and ink.** A sheet of `--paper` on the grid, a 1.5 px ink edge, the house offset shadow. Nothing glossy, nothing rounded, nothing glowing.
- **Two accent inks at most.** Orange (`conc`) is what's being tested; blue (`dif`) is the contrasting term or the answer. Grey is out of focus. Colour always means the same thing on every page.
- **The hand.** Mono uppercase labels for structure, sans for the main noun, serif for reading, the italic hand for one-line notes. Labels sit in clear space; nothing crosses a line.
- **Real things, measured.** Every figure shows a real legal object (a statute's text, a vote, a date, a form, a map, a court) or measures something real (a quorum, a prazo, a sequence). Positions, lengths and counts mean something.
- **Calm.** Motion only when the movement is the content: a line being drawn, a vote being counted, a deadline running.
- **One composition for every screen**, both themes, print.
- **The gates:** Figure Library 5-point test, frame fits the drawing, negative space never dominates (4×4 ≥ 10/16), breakscan 0/0.

## What is free (the role)

Everything else is cast per lesson:

- **The object.** Which real thing from this lesson's law is on the sheet. Controle 01 measures a right's text with a ruler; another lesson might show a Diário Oficial page, a plenary seating plan, a stamp, a docket, a ballot.
- **The form.** Ruler, ledger, map, seat plan, facsimile, calendar, cross-section, docket, ballot sheet, tally, a strip of film frames. The form comes from the object, not from a menu.
- **The composition.** Portrait or landscape, one object or a pair, a sequence of panels or a single sheet.
- **The behaviour.** What the reader does: count, measure, compare, read, find, decide, trace.

## The three layers

| Layer | Who decides | What it is | Changes how often |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The house** | CEO (Design Direction) | paper, inks, type roles, label rules, frame, motion rules, gates | almost never |
| **The course's world** | CEO, one page per course | the materials that course's law is made of, which its figures draw from | once per course |
| **The lesson's cast** | the blueprint author, approved by the panel and the CEO | one figure idea per claim, born from this lesson's move | every lesson |

### The course's world (one page per course; drafts below)

Each course has a world: the physical stuff its law produces, which gives its figures a family resemblance without repeating any of them. Like the props department of one film.

- **Controle de Constitucionalidade:** the statute's printed text, the Diário Oficial page, the STF plenary (eleven seats), the vote count, the docket with its dates, the quorum, the stamp of promulgação, the clock of a session. *Signature:* text measured against text.
- **Teoria do Delito:** the inquérito folder, the timeline of a single act, the tipo penal's elements as a checklist on a form, the line between dolo and culpa as a field, the courtroom. *Signature:* one act examined through successive filters.
- **Teoria Geral dos Contratos:** the contract itself, clause by clause, signatures, the dates of offer and acceptance, the ledger of performance, the receipt. *Signature:* the paper the parties signed.
- **Direito Latino-Americano:** the atlas plate, borders by date, regimes on a timeline, courts as tallies. *Signature:* real geography and real chronology (already the bar).
- **Processo Civil I:** the petição, the autos, the calendar of prazos, the clerk's stamp, the order of acts. *Signature:* the procedural calendar.
- **Metodologia Jurídica:** the glossed manuscript page, the commentary layered on the text, the library shelf. *Signature:* text, gloss, commentary.
- **Direito Constitucional I:** the Constitution's architecture (titles, chapters, articles as a building plan), the three branches as institutions with seats and powers, the amendment record. *Signature:* the Constitution as a built structure.

## Casting a lesson's figure (what the blueprint delivers in A7)

For each figure the blueprint names:

1. **The move.** The one thing the reader must be able to do after this section, in a sentence. "Tell a forward-looking ruling from anulabilidade."
2. **The cast list.** At least three candidate depictions, each from a different form family and drawn from the course's world, each in one sentence of what's on the sheet.
3. **The uniqueness test**, applied to each candidate:
   - **Swap test:** could this exact figure illustrate a different lesson by changing its labels? Then it's generic; reject it.
   - **Ledger test:** has this course already used this form for a different idea? Then it's a repeat unless the blueprint argues a deliberate callback (one per course at most). Same form within one lesson is continuity and welcome (Aula 01's ruler runs hero → Fig. 3).
   - **Object test:** is the real object of THIS lesson's law on the sheet (its article, its case, its numbers)?
   - **Verb test:** what does the reader do with it, and what do they know afterwards that the text alone didn't give them?
4. **The choice and the sentence that sells it.** "We cast X because only X makes Y visible."
5. **The sheet, described.** What is on it, top to bottom, with real data: every label, every number, which ink is the tested term. Specific enough that two different builders would draw nearly the same thing.

### The casting ledger

`work/figure-qc/casting-ledger.md` lists every live figure: course, lesson, object, form, move. The blueprint author reads the course's rows before casting. The panel rejects a cast whose form repeats a different idea in the same course without an argued callback.

## Six casts, to set the bar

| Lesson | Move | Generic cast (rejected) | The cast | Why only this works |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Controle 01** · Conceito | measure a law against the right it touches | pyramid of norms | **a ruler** graduated in the words of art. 5º, XVI ("livre … aviso prévio | autorização … proibição"), each law a strip laid against it | the right's own text becomes the scale; past "independentemente de autorização" is hatched |
| **Controle 02** · Pressupostos | see why rigidity makes a conflict a defect | three boxes: supremacia / rigidez / órgão | **two vote sheets side by side**: an emenda needs 308 of 513 deputies and 49 of 81 senators, twice each; a lei ordinária needs a simple majority of those present. The same 300 votes pass one sheet and fail the other | rigidity IS a number; the reader sees that 300 votes make a law but can't touch the Constitution |
| **Controle 08** · Nulidade e modulação | tell what a ruling reaches back to | a timeline with "ex tunc ← → ex nunc" arrows | **the Mira Estrela council's record book**: the legislatura's sessions as a ledger of acts; the STF's line cuts *forward* at the next legislatura, while the textbook nullity line would have struck the whole column back to the law's birth | the ruling's real effect shown on the real acts it would have voided |
| **Contratos** · Formação | find the moment the contract exists | flow chart: proposta → aceitação → contrato | **an envelope's journey on a calendar**: the offer posted, the acceptance posted, received; the dates the law cares about circled, and the day the contract exists in orange | the theories of formation ARE dates; the calendar makes them countable |
| **Delito** · Dolo eventual × culpa consciente | place an act on the line between two mental states | two columns of definitions | **a field** whose axes are "foresaw the result" and "accepted it", with three real cases placed as points and the boundary drawn | the distinction is two variables; only a field shows both at once |
| **Processo** · Prazos | count a deadline | a box saying "15 dias úteis" | **the real month** with weekends and holidays shaded, day 1 and the last day marked | the reader does the count themselves (already the course's best figure) |

## Roles

- **CEO:** writes this brief and each course's world page; approves casts at the gate; keeps the ledger honest. Never draws.
- **Blueprint author (S5a):** casts every figure (steps 1–5) in A7.
- **Panel:** applies the four tests; rejects generic and repeated casts.
- **Builder (S5):** draws exactly the described sheet with the kit's primitives and the house rules, and may improve composition but never the cast.
- **Kit:** primitives and craft (type, strokes, leading, labels, hatching, motion), never ready-made layouts. A new component is a new material for a course's world, not a template.
