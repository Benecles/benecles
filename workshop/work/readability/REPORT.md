# READ-1 — decision report

## Decision frame

The live desktop reading column is `min(1,083px, viewport − 96px)`, giving 1,083px at a 1,440px viewport. The former 76ch value is historical. Pixels cannot be converted to characters without measuring the rendered passage in its actual face. The evidence below comes from the six research memos; each memo links its primary studies, standards, or documentation. This comparison specimen is for a chairman's decision and does not itself justify a site-wide change.

## 1. Measure

**Finding and evidence grade.** A controlled screen study found its best comprehension at 55 characters per line among the tested 25, 55, and 100 character lines; that does not establish a continuous optimum or a 50–75-character band. NN/G's 50–75 range is expert convention for chunking, not a controlled estimate. Visual-span research supplies a perceptual mechanism, not a paragraph-width target. The cited Arial experiment did not vary line length. No named source tests this face, 19px, Portuguese legal prose, or this reader population. ([Q1 memo](research/q1-measure.md); [Dyson & Haselgrove](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581901904586); [NN/G](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/chunking/); [Legge et al.](https://legge.psych.umn.edu/sites/legge.psych.umn.edu/files/2020-08/Legge%20Cheung%20Yu%20Chung%20Lee%20Owens%202007%20The%20case%20for%20the%20visual%20span%20as%20a%20sensory%20bottleneck%20in%20reading.pdf); [Rello et al.](https://www.pielot.org/pubs/Rello2016-Fontsize.pdf))

**CSS implication / specimen.** Keep the live width as baseline. Add one narrower alternative whose measured rendered line length is near 55 characters in the current face at 19px; record the measured result for the chosen passage. Treat that as a comparison point, not a universal target. Do not revive 76ch or infer a character count from 1,083px.

## 2. Body size and leading

**Finding and evidence grade.** Controlled results are mixed and do not establish an ideal size or line-height at this measure. In 28 participants with dyslexia reading short Wikipedia excerpts, larger sizes improved several outcomes up to about 18–22pt, while spacing effects were weak or mixed; those point sizes do not translate directly to CSS pixels. An eye-tracking study of 36 native English-speaking undergraduates without known reading difficulties found subtle processing benefits and less accurate return sweeps with wider spacing in four-line, 19pt monospaced excerpts. Dyson's review describes extra leading for long lines as typographic convention and says systematic joint screen tests were lacking. None tests this site's face, Portuguese prose, 1,083px measure, or sustained legal reading. ([Q2 memo](research/q2-size-leading.md); [Rello et al.](https://pielot.org/pubs/Rello2013-W4A-SizeMatters.pdf); [Vasilev et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10600290/); [Dyson](https://doi.org/10.1080/01449290410001715714))

**CSS implication / specimen.** Preserve 19px as baseline. Provide a single alternative with modestly larger size and more open leading, changing both together only if the comparison is explicitly treated as a combined preference test; ideally expose them separately to learn which change matters. The evidence does not require line-height to rise with measure or supply numeric CSS values. Preserve browser zoom and text resizing.

## 3. Contrast and paper

**Finding and evidence grade.** Controlled evidence does not establish that warm paper is more comfortable than neutral light gray or that pure black on white is best. A 20-observer tablet preference study favored black on light gray across ambient surrounds, but measured comfort ratings, not reading performance, and did not establish a warm-hue advantage. Polarity findings vary by task and setup: proofreading studies favored dark-on-light, one follow-up found the effect disappeared when display luminance was matched, and two 2023 studies found faster light-mode performance. The site's current light and dark token pairs calculate to 13.32:1 and 13.96:1, above WCAG AA's 4.5:1 ordinary-text threshold; conformance is not comfort. Low-vision readers with cloudy ocular media are a documented exception to a general polarity rule. ([Q3 memo](research/q3-contrast-paper.md); [Huang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/col.22259); [Buchner & Baumgartner](https://doi.org/10.1080/00140130701306413); [Buchner et al.](https://doi.org/10.1080/00140130802641635); [Palmén et al.](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3581552); [Rubin & Legge](https://doi.org/10.1016/0042-6989(89)90175-2); [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum/); [current theme tokens](https://github.com/Benecles/ordenacoes-filipinas/blob/main/assets/course.css#L9-L18))

**CSS implication / specimen.** Keep current paper and ink as baseline; retain a light/dark mode toggle. If the chair wants a paper comparison, use one neutral light-gray surface with text color chosen to preserve at least 4.5:1, and label it a preference comparison. Do not claim a comfort benefit from WCAG conformance. Do not add dark-mode weight compensation as an evidence-based fix: the cited grade study found no dark-mode fluency benefit. No warm-paper superiority variant is warranted by these sources.

## 4. Typeface

**Finding and evidence grade.** Controlled work and review evidence do not name a universally superior reading serif. X-height offers a plausible legibility mechanism but does not isolate typeface design or establish a winner. A matched paperback comparison found no significant speed or error difference between Times New Roman and Helvetica; a limited low-vision letter-identification study found an interaction between stroke contrast and serif presence, not a ranking for typical readers. Optical sizing and font axes are documented design features, not reader-outcome evidence. No located head-to-head study compares Source Serif 4, Literata, Newsreader, or a specified Charter build for sustained Portuguese reading at 19px and this measure. ([Q4 memo](research/q4-typeface.md); [Legge & Bigelow](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3428264/); [Tyrrell et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9804255/); [Minakata et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2022.103810); [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/font-optical-sizing); [Source Serif 4 metadata](https://github.com/google/fonts/blob/main/ofl/sourceserif4/METADATA.pb); [Newsreader](https://github.com/productiontype/Newsreader))

**CSS implication / specimen.** Keep the current face as baseline. Compare Source Serif 4 and Newsreader as single-variable family switches, with passage, 19px size, measure, leading, colors, and weight fixed. Use `font-optical-sizing: auto` where supported; verify computed behavior and actual served glyphs, including Portuguese accents. Literata is an optional third candidate. Do not include Charter until the exact free build and character coverage are identified. Fixed weight keeps this separate from MOT-1's hover-weight question.

## 5. Paragraph rhythm and alignment

**Finding and evidence grade.** A controlled 1979 study of 72 adults found faster sentence-verification responses for meaningful phrase segmentation and indentation, but did not compare paragraph gaps with first-line indents or study web reading. Two older computer-text experiments conflict on justification: one found slower reading with fill justification and no comprehension difference; an eight-experiment sequence found no general difference. A review also reports mixed results. These do not establish a universal ragged-right comprehension advantage. Browser documentation describes `text-wrap: pretty` as a layout hint, not an orphan/widow guarantee; `hyphens: auto` depends on declared language and browser dictionary support, with browser-specific breaks. No direct study here tests these choices in Brazilian Portuguese legal web prose. ([Q5 memo](research/q5-rhythm.md); [Frase & Schwartz](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.71.2.197); [Trollip & Sales](https://doi.org/10.1177/001872088602800204); [Coll et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0378-7206(98)00059-7); [Lonsdale review](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/82895/11/VisibleLanguage-48-3_28-67-Lonsdale-TypeFeatures.pdf); [MDN `text-wrap`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/text-wrap); [MDN `hyphens`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/hyphens))

**CSS implication / specimen.** Use left alignment, modest paragraph gap, and no first-line indent as a convention-led visual condition; avoid adding a full blank line on top of existing leading. This may be the baseline if it matches the live page; otherwise offer one rhythm alternative. `text-wrap: pretty` may be progressive enhancement. Set `lang="pt-BR"` before testing optional `hyphens: auto`; inspect target-browser breaks. Neither behavior is a proven comprehension intervention.

## 6. Distraction and navigation

**Finding and evidence grade.** No direct controlled evidence located tests graph-paper texture behind prose, persistent progress bars, sticky-control occlusion, or bold density. A 2024 eye-tracking study using two science texts and six simple static peripheral distractions found no significant effect in its sample; it does not test animation or textured backgrounds. A review of 38 hypertext studies found link decisions can raise visual and decision demands, with effects depending on reader, task, and link structure; another web study found readers use links as navigation markers. Heading experiments support topic structure for some memory and processing outcomes. Wikimedia's small qualitative usability study (18 readers/editors across three regions) found positive reactions to a contents list for page structure and navigation, not comprehension gains. ([Q6 memo](research/q6-distraction-navigation.md); [Ronconi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcal.13106); [DeStefano & LeFevre](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2005.08.012); [Fitzsimmons et al.](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0239134); [Lorch et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1135-755X(13)70011-3); [Hyönä & Lorch](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2004.01.001); [Wikimedia testing](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Reading/Web/Desktop_Improvements/Repository/Sticky_Header_and_Table_of_Contents_User_Testing))

**CSS implication / specimen.** Keep a grid only at very low contrast or confined to margins; this is a cautious visual choice, not a tested readability result. Avoid looping or flashing motion beside prose. Keep contextual links visible, sparse, and relevant; use semantic headings and a contents list when lesson length warrants. A short optional sticky contents control may be compared for navigation utility, with anchored headings given `scroll-margin-block-start`; no comprehension claim follows. A progress cue is optional orientation feedback. Evidence does not set a bold-density threshold, so do not invent one; reserve bold for semantic emphasis.

## Specimen conditions for the next stage

Use the same real passage and viewport for every condition. The minimum useful comparison set is:

1. **Baseline:** live 1,083px cap, current 19px face, current leading, colors, and prose treatment.
2. **Measure:** one narrower column measured near 55 rendered characters per line in the current face at 19px; report actual line lengths.
3. **Size/leading:** one modestly larger/open alternative with all other variables fixed; expose size and leading separately if the specimen can support it.
4. **Typeface:** Source Serif 4 and Newsreader switches, one family at a time, holding other typography constant and checking actual Portuguese glyph coverage.
5. **Paper:** optional neutral light-gray comparison against current paper, preserving 4.5:1 ordinary-text contrast. Keep dark mode selectable as the current alternate.
6. **Rhythm/navigation:** only if the live page differs from the evidence-led convention above, compare modest paragraph spacing/left alignment or a compact contents control. Do not fabricate an evidence-based result for grid, progress, or bold density.

The chairman chooses what looks and reads best in the specimen. Any later site-wide change needs a separate decision and implementation step.
