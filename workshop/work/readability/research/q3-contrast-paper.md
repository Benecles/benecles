# READ-1 · Q3 — Contrast and paper

## Finding

The available evidence does not establish that warm paper and ink improve reading over neutral off-white/black, or that pure black on white is the most comfortable combination. A controlled tablet preference study found black text on light gray most comfortable across four ambient-lit viewing conditions; it did not test a warm cream or measure long-form reading performance. ([Huang et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/col.22259))

For light versus dark polarity, findings are mixed by task and experimental setup. Controlled proofreading studies report an advantage for dark-on-light, while another controlled study found that this advantage disappeared when overall display luminance was matched; a 2023 pair of reading studies again found faster reading in light mode, including paragraph reading on phones. ([Buchner & Baumgartner, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1080/00140130701306413); [Buchner, Mayr & Brandt, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1080/00140130802641635); [Palmén, Gilbert & Crossland, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3581552))

The present course palette already uses softened, warm light mode and a dark charcoal night mode. Applying WCAG’s luminance formula to the course CSS tokens gives 13.32:1 for `#1d2530` on `#f2eee3` and 13.96:1 for `#e5e3da` on `#14171d`; both exceed WCAG 2.2 AA’s 4.5:1 threshold for ordinary text. These figures establish contrast conformance for these pairs, not reading comfort. ([course.css](https://github.com/Benecles/ordenacoes-filipinas/blob/main/assets/course.css#L9-L18); [WCAG 2.2, SC 1.4.3 and formula](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum/))

## Evidence and quality

- **Controlled study — paper tint / preference, limited:** In a paired-comparison psychophysical study, 20 observers rated 20 text/background lightness combinations on a tablet across a dark surround and four illuminated surrounds; black text on light gray was rated most comfortable under ambient illumination. It measured subjective comfort, not reading speed or comprehension, and the abstract does not establish a benefit for warm hue specifically. ([Huang et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/col.22259))
- **Controlled study — polarity, mixed:** Buchner and Baumgartner’s experiments found better proofreading with dark text on light background across illumination and chromaticity conditions, without corresponding differences in reported eyestrain or fatigue. Their follow-up independently varied polarity and total display luminance: with contrast matched, polarity had no significant effect, while higher display luminance improved error detection. ([2007 study](https://doi.org/10.1080/00140130701306413); [2009 study](https://doi.org/10.1080/00140130802641635))
- **Controlled study — reading mode and grade:** Two studies with 459 total participants found faster performance in light mode than dark mode in both word-pair and paragraph tasks. Increasing variable-font grade in dark mode did not improve fluency; grade changes had no detected paragraph-fluency effect. The tested font, sizes, devices, tasks, and English text limit direct transfer to this site’s serif Portuguese prose. ([Palmén et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3581552))
- **Controlled study — low vision exception:** Rubin and Legge tested 19 low-vision readers; readers with cloudy ocular media could benefit from reversed polarity, which the authors attributed to intraocular light scatter. This is evidence for an individual clinical exception, not a general dark-mode advantage for readers with typical vision. ([Rubin & Legge, 1989](https://doi.org/10.1016/0042-6989(89)90175-2))
- **Accessibility standard, normative rather than comfort evidence:** WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3 requires at least 4.5:1 for ordinary text and 3:1 for large text. W3C explains these as minimum conformance thresholds and cautions that thin fonts may appear fainter despite nominally passing; neither the ratio nor passing the criterion predicts comfort or reading speed. ([W3C Understanding SC 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum/))
- **Expert convention / opinion:** “Halation” is used in typography discussions for apparent spreading of light glyphs against dark fields, but these sources do not demonstrate that it is a universal reader response. Palmén et al. tested grade as a possible compensation; grade adjustment did not improve dark-mode fluency in their experiments. A general dark-mode weight-compensation rule is therefore not supported by this study. ([Palmén et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3581552))
- **Field data:** The cited evidence here is experimental or normative; it supplies no large field dataset showing that warm paper, pure white, or a particular night-mode palette improves sustained reading comfort.

## Limits

The studies use different outcomes—proofreading accuracy, word-pair speed, paragraph fluency, or comfort ratings—and different screens, luminance levels, typefaces, languages, and exposure conditions; their results should not be collapsed into one universal ranking. ([Buchner & Baumgartner, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1080/00140130701306413); [Buchner et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1080/00140130802641635); [Huang et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/col.22259); [Palmén et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3581552)) WCAG contrast uses specified foreground/background relative luminance and does not encode surrounding light, screen brightness, reader preference, ocular scatter, or prolonged comfort. ([W3C Understanding SC 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum/))

## CSS implication

Keep both modes as selectable options in the later specimen. The current `--paper`/`--ink` pairs already clear AA for ordinary text by the WCAG calculation above; do not infer from that pass that either is comfortable for everyone. If exploring a softer light surface, compare it as a specimen variant while preserving sufficient luminance contrast. Keep night-mode text/background contrast compliant; do not automatically increase font weight to offset presumed halation, because the available controlled grade study found no dark-mode fluency benefit. ([course.css](https://github.com/Benecles/ordenacoes-filipinas/blob/main/assets/course.css#L9-L18); [W3C Understanding SC 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum/); [Palmén et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3581552))
