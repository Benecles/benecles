# Statutes table — primary-source check for Claude lessons 01–08

**Checked 24 Sep 2026** against the currently published compiled texts on Planalto. This is a private review aid, not lesson copy. Rules below are paraphrases; consult the linked provision for the full text.

Sources: [Civil Code, Law 10.406/2002 (compiled)](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/2002/l10406compilada.htm) · [Consumer Code, Law 8.078/1990 (compiled)](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8078compilado.htm).

| CDC 2–3 | Consumer may be a natural or legal person acquiring/using a product or service as final recipient; an intervening collective is equated to a consumer. Supplier definition covers public/private, domestic/foreign persons and unincorporated entities conducting the listed production/trade/service activities. “Legal person can be consumer” is qualified by final-recipient status; the text alone does not settle borderline business-use cases. | 01 (contract in CDC; consumer / supplier) | [CDC arts. 2–3](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8078compilado.htm) |
| CDC 6 | Core consumer rights include clear information, protection against abusive practices/terms, modification of disproportionate terms or review for supervening excessive burden, and prevention/compensation of harm. Burden shifting is a judicial option when the statutory plausibility or vulnerability condition is met, not automatic. | 01, 03, 08 (consumer frame; fairness; adhesion/mass contracting) | [CDC art. 6](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8078compilado.htm) |
| CDC 47, 51 | Consumer contract clauses are read in the consumer’s favor. Article 51 invalidates specified abusive terms, including terms imposing exaggerated disadvantage or conflicting with good faith/equity; the list is non-exhaustive. This consumer rule is distinct from CC 423, which applies to ambiguity/contradiction in an adhesion contract. | 01, 03, 08 | [CDC arts. 47, 51](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8078compilado.htm) |
| CC 112–113 | Interpret declarations by the intention embodied in them rather than literal wording alone; interpret legal transactions in light of good faith and local usage. Current art. 113 §1 supplies listed interpretive criteria (including later conduct, market practice, good faith, and, if identifiable, the non-drafter-favorable meaning); §2 permits agreed interpretive/integration rules. These paragraphs were added by Law 13.874/2019. | 01 (concept/transaction), 03 (good faith), 05 (offer meaning); principally P2 interpretation under the architecture boundary | [CC arts. 112–113](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/2002/l10406compilada.htm) |
| CC 187 | A rights-holder commits an unlawful act by manifestly exceeding limits imposed by the right’s economic/social purpose, good faith, or good customs. Do not reduce this to a free-standing general balancing formula: the statutory trigger is manifest excess in exercising a right. | 02–03 (contractual principles; good faith/social function) | [CC art. 187](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/2002/l10406compilada.htm) |
| CC 421, 421-A | Contractual freedom operates within the contract’s social function. Private contractual relations are governed by minimal intervention and exceptional review. Civil and business contracts are presumed equal/symmetric unless concrete elements justify otherwise, subject to special statutory regimes; risk allocation is respected and revision is exceptional and limited. Both provisions reflect Law 13.874/2019. The presumption does not displace consumer-law regimes. | 02–03, 08 (freedom/social function; parity and adhesion classification) | [CC arts. 421–421-A](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/2002/l10406compilada.htm) |
| CC 422 | Parties must observe probity and good faith at conclusion and during performance. The text does not expressly name negotiation-stage duties; pre-contractual liability should be presented as a doctrinal/jurisprudential application, not as words expressly stated in 422. | 03–04 (good faith; negotiations/pre-contractual responsibility) | [CC art. 422](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/2002/l10406compilada.htm) |
| CC 423–425 | In adhesion contracts, ambiguity/contradiction is construed for the adherent (423); advance waiver of a right inherent in the transaction is void (424); atypical contracts are permitted subject to the Code’s general rules (425). 423 is narrower than a blanket rule that every adhesion term favors the adherent. | 08 (adhesion/atypical classification); 03 as interpretive principle | [CC arts. 423–425](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/2002/l10406compilada.htm) |
| CC 427–429 | Proposal binds the proposer unless its terms, transaction nature, or circumstances show otherwise (427). Article 428 lists when an offer lapses depending on presence/absence, deadlines, and timely retraction; phone/similar communication counts as present for 428 I. Public offer is proposal only if it contains essential terms, subject to circumstances/usages; revocation by same publicity route requires that revocation power was reserved (429). | 04–05 (process/formation; offer) | [CC arts. 427–429](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/2002/l10406compilada.htm) |
| CC 430–435 | Unforeseen late receipt of acceptance requires prompt notice, failing which damages may follow (430); late or modified acceptance is a new proposal (431); silence concludes only transactions where express acceptance is not customary or was dispensed with, if refusal does not arrive in time (432). Acceptance retraction, dispatch rule for absent parties, and its three statutory exceptions are in 433–434. Place of contract is where proposed (435). Avoid teaching “silence always accepts” or “dispatch always controls” without these limits. | 05 (acceptance; silence; time/place) | [CC arts. 430–435](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/2002/l10406compilada.htm) |
| CC 417–420 | Arras given at contract conclusion are returned/credited on performance (417). On non-performance, the innocent party may dissolve and retain the deposit if giver defaults, or dissolve and recover equivalent plus monetary adjustment, interest and attorney fees if recipient defaults (418, current wording); supplemental damages require proof of greater loss, or performance may be sought with arras as minimum compensation (419). If a right to withdraw is stipulated, arras are solely compensatory and no additional damages are due (420). Art. 418 was amended by Law 14.905/2024; those provisions took effect 60 days after publication (not the 2019 wording sometimes found in older materials). | 06 (arras); 07 (classification examples involving onerous/aleatory agreements only as relevant) | [CC arts. 417–420](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/2002/l10406compilada.htm); [Law 14.905/2024, arts. 2 and 5](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2023-2026/2024/lei/l14905.htm) |
| CC 439–440 | A person promising another’s act is liable for damages if the third person does not perform; a narrow spouse/consent/regime exception appears in the sole paragraph of 439. Under 440, no obligation attaches to the promisor if the third person had already bound themselves and then defaults. Distinguish promise of another’s future act from stipulation in favor of a third party (436–438). | 06 (promise of third-party act) | [CC arts. 439–440](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/2002/l10406compilada.htm) |
| CC 462–466 | Preliminary contract must contain all essential terms except form (462). Without withdrawal clause, a party may demand the definitive contract; judge may supply the defaulting party’s will unless the obligation’s nature prevents it (463–464). Non-performance can support dissolution and damages (465). A unilateral promise lapses unless the creditor accepts within the stated or reasonably fixed period (466). Do not state that registration under 463 sole paragraph is invariably a condition of validity; the article says it “shall” be registered, while art. 462 states the formation essentials. | 06 (preliminary contract) | [CC arts. 462–466](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/2002/l10406compilada.htm) |
| CC 476–477 | In bilateral contracts neither party may demand the other’s performance before performing their own (476). If, after contract formation, one party’s assets diminish enough to jeopardize or cast doubt on its promised performance, the other may withhold its own until performance or adequate security (477). The second is a distinct insecurity defense, not merely another description of ordinary non-performance. | 07 (exceptio / insecurity defense) | [CC arts. 476–477](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/2002/l10406compilada.htm) |
| CC 441–446 | Hidden defects in a commutative contract that make the thing unfit or reduce its value allow rejection or price reduction; the rule also reaches onerous donations (441–442). Seller’s knowledge affects damages (443); defect must pre-exist delivery for liability if the thing later perishes (444). Decadence periods and discovery/guarantee rules are specifically set out in 445–446. Avoid implying every defect is redhibitory or omitting the time bars. | 07 (commutative × aleatory; redhibitory defects) | [CC arts. 441–446](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/2002/l10406compilada.htm) |
| CC 447–457 | In onerous contracts the transferor warrants against eviction, including public auction (447). Parties may expressly adjust/exclude the warranty (448), but 449 preserves recovery of price if the buyer did not know or assume the risk. Article 450 sets default recovery; 455 distinguishes substantial partial eviction from insubstantial; 457 bars claim where buyer knew the thing was another’s or litigated. **Art. 456 is repealed** by CPC Law 13.105/2015. | 07 (onerous × gratuitous; eviction) | [CC arts. 447–457](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/2002/l10406compilada.htm) |
| CC 458–461 | Aleatory-contract risk allocation controls payment consequences for future things/facts or existing things exposed to risk. Arts. 458–460 have distinct risk patterns and qualifications, including no alienation/refund if nothing comes into existence under 459 sole paragraph; 461 permits annulment for dolo if the counterparty knew the risk had already materialized. Do not collapse all aleatory contracts into “uncertainty means full price.” | 07–08 (commutative/aleatory; sorting examples) | [CC arts. 458–461](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/2002/l10406compilada.htm) |

## Review notes for the orchestrator

- The architecture note sets the exam boundary at lessons/units 1–5, while its deliverable map includes eight lessons and the B4 claim review extends through articles for lessons 06–07. Keep the statute table useful to all eight lessons, but mark lessons 06–08 as course coverage rather than automatically P1 exam scope unless the professor’s materials say otherwise.
- The architecture’s phrasing that good faith applies through the “process” can be pedagogically sound, but CC 422 itself says conclusion and execution. If attributing negotiation-stage duties to 422, flag this as doctrine/case law rather than literal statutory text.
- Its 2019 source discussion may be stale for arras: CC 418 now contains the 2024 amendments (updated monetary adjustment, interest and attorney fees language for recovery by the giver when recipient defaults). Check any slide/key formulation against the current provision and the exam’s prescribed source vintage.
- Distinguish CC 423’s ambiguity/contradiction rule for adhesion terms from CDC 47’s consumer-favorable interpretation; do not treat CC 421-A’s parity presumption as displacing special consumer legislation.
- CC 456 is shown as revoked in the compiled current Code. Avoid relying on its former evicção procedural text as current law.
- The plan’s “redibitórios” link under commutative/aleatory classification is only a teaching association: arts. 441–442 apply to commutative contracts and onerous donations, with hidden-defect and deadline requirements. Eviction likewise has defined conditions in arts. 447–457; it is not a blanket consequence of every onerous contract.
- No specific inaccurate article claim appears in `10-claude-architecture-and-batches.md`; it names the article ranges for review rather than stating their rules. These are the guardrails most likely to prevent overgeneralization during drafting.
