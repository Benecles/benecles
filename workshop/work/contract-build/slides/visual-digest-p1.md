# Contract slides: visual digest, pass 1

Read-only review of five distinct source pages at full readable resolution. The two PDF sources remain untouched. The book-strip imagery is treated as recurring decoration rather than course content.

## Aula 1 — PDF page 15

**Visible elements and position.** The upper-left heading reads “2. O contrato como operação econômica - elemento substancial”. Beneath it, aligned toward the left, is “Linguagem comum: o ‘negócio’.” The main field places “Operação econômica” above “Especulação econômica”, with a large red not-equal sign between them. A narrow bookshelf image runs vertically along the right edge; a red block and small white down arrow sit at the upper-right.

**Conceptual meaning.** The slide distinguishes a contract as an economic operation from the narrower idea of economic speculation. The not-equal sign rejects treating the two as synonymous.

**Contribution beyond selectable text.** The prominent inequality turns the distinction into the whole lesson of the page. Without it, extracted text lists two expressions but does not tell the reader to compare them as non-equivalent.

**Redraw specification.** On a warm drafting-paper field, set two graphite labels on separate baselines, centered in the content area: “Operação econômica” above and “Especulação econômica” below. Put a compact red ≠ between them; use a fine blue construction line to align the three marks. Omit the bookshelf and navigation arrow.

**Misreading risk.** Replacing ≠ with a divider, equals sign, or causal arrow would change the proposition. The hierarchy should not imply that speculation is a subtype or consequence of every economic operation.

## Aula 1 — PDF page 16

**Visible elements and position.** The same upper-left heading, “2. O contrato como operação econômica - elemento substancial”, appears. In the broad open center, “Circulação de riqueza” sits above a red downward arrow; “Função do contrato.” is below. The right-edge bookshelf and upper-right red navigation block repeat.

**Conceptual meaning.** Contract function is presented in relation to the circulation of wealth: the slide directs the reader from circulation toward the contract’s function.

**Contribution beyond selectable text.** The arrow supplies the direction and connection. The extracted labels alone do not establish whether the two ideas are alternatives, a definition, or a sequence.

**Redraw specification.** Use a vertical graphite flow: “Circulação de riqueza” → “Função do contrato”, with a slim red downward arrow centered between labels and a blue alignment rule. Retain generous white space and the drafting-sheet paper tone; remove recurring decorative furniture.

**Misreading risk.** The arrow indicates an explanatory progression, not necessarily a complete causal claim. Keep it one-way and downward as shown; do not turn the relationship into equivalence.

## Aula 1 — PDF page 17

**Visible elements and position.** The heading changes to “3. Conceito normativo” at upper left. A large muted-red oval callout fills the central field. Inside it, centered black text reads: “O termo contrato é usado tanto para designar a materialidade da operação econômica quanto para nomear a sua formalização jurídica e as consequências da inserção desta operação econômica no mundo jurídico.” Red vertical edge furniture and a small downward navigation arrow remain at the page edges.

**Conceptual meaning.** “Contrato” has two connected uses here: the economic operation itself and its legal formalization, including the consequences of bringing that operation into the legal sphere.

**Contribution beyond selectable text.** The oval groups the full definition as one emphasized unit. The page is a definition callout, not a process diagram: it does not map separate stages or show a directional relationship among the clauses.

**Redraw specification.** Preserve the definition verbatim as a centered graphite paragraph in a restrained rounded callout, using a very light red wash or a red outline and one small blue drafting accent. Keep the heading separate above it. In the site version, favor a clean text callout over reproducing the original large filled oval.

**Misreading risk.** Do not let the enclosing shape suggest that legal formalization is an optional stage or that the “consequences” apply only to the formalization. The wording links the term to the materiality, its formalization, and the consequences of insertion into law.

## Aula 1 — PDF page 21

**Visible elements and position.** Under “3. Conceito normativo”, a large muted-red oval contains a centered quotation block. The text reads: “Há contratos que, por estarem fora daquele espaço especialmente tutelado por normas de direito cogente, admitem a possibilidade de determinação do conteúdo contratual, ou, como registra Enzo Roppo, a possibilidade de ‘definir que composição, que arranjo recíproco receberão os interesses das partes, coenvolvidos na operação econômica e a que o contrato é chamado a dar veste e vinculatividade jurídica’”. The source rendering has a spelling/character extraction defect around “conteúdo contratual”; visually the intended word is “contratual.” The recurring edge blocks and down arrow are present.

**Conceptual meaning.** Some contracts outside the specially protected sphere of mandatory rules allow the parties to determine contractual content: they can arrange the reciprocal interests involved in the underlying economic operation, which the contract gives legal form and binding force.

**Contribution beyond selectable text.** The oval marks the passage as a cited doctrinal explanation and visually separates it from the preceding definition. It does not encode a comparison or flow. Treat it as a quotation callout, not a diagram.

**Redraw specification.** Use a compact quotation card on drafting paper, with a fine graphite border, a small red opening quote mark, and a blue attribution cue for Enzo Roppo. Typeset the full quote as readable text and preserve the quotation marks; do not recreate the large opaque oval.

**Misreading risk.** Avoid implying that party choice is unrestricted in all contracts. The qualification is explicit: the contracts described are outside the specially protected area of mandatory rules. Keep the attribution and the quotation’s link to reciprocal interests and juridical binding force.

## Aula 2 — PDF page 19

**Duplicate reference.** This page’s sinalagma relationship is duplicated in `Slides Aula 3.pdf`, PDF page 7.

**Visible elements and position.** The upper-left heading reads “5. Princípios contratuais”. Centered below it is the uppercase label “SINALAGMA CONTRATUAL”. A centered line reads “Sinalagma -> Reciprocidade.” Beneath that, a centered paragraph defines it as “Estrutura de vantagens e de ônus recíprocos, característica dos contratos bilaterais e comutativos, nos quais há relativa correspondência entre os mútuos riscos e benefícios.” The final centered line contrasts “Sinalagma genético (ex: lesão) x sinalagma funcional (ex: revisão).” The narrow bookshelf edge and top-right navigation arrow are decorative.

**Conceptual meaning.** Sinalagma is reciprocity: bilateral/comutative contracts have corresponding burdens and advantages, with a relative correspondence between reciprocal risks and benefits. The final line names two forms: genetic (example: lesion) and functional (example: revision).

**Contribution beyond selectable text.** The centered title and nested text hierarchy make the definition the organizing concept; `->` explicitly links the term to reciprocity, and `x` visibly separates the two named forms. There is no actual two-party diagram, paired set of arrows, or visual account of how genetic and functional sinalagma differ. The triage’s statement that the page “lays out” reciprocal performance is directionally right but could overstate the graphic: the page is primarily a structured text slide.

**Redraw specification.** Redraw as a small conceptual sheet: heading in graphite; a central “Sinalagma = reciprocidade” node; below, a compact two-way exchange motif (matching arrows between two party labels) with “vantagens / ônus” and “riscos / benefícios” on opposite sides. Finish with two clearly separated labels, “genético — lesão” and “funcional — revisão,” connected to the central concept with light blue rules and restrained red accents. Add no substantive party labels absent from the slide unless the surrounding course text establishes them.

**Misreading risk.** “Relativa correspondência” does not mean exact equality of value or perfectly balanced outcomes. The genetic/functional distinction is stated with examples, but the slide does not define those forms; avoid using the redraw to invent a distinction beyond the supplied course explanation.

## Triage comparison

The five specified pages confirm the triage’s descriptions of p. 15’s inequality, p. 16’s downward relation, and p. 17/p. 21’s oval definition/quotation callouts. The only refinement is Aula 2 p. 19: its instructional structure is conveyed mostly through centered typography and the explicit `->` / `x` symbols, rather than a drawn reciprocal-performance diagram. Redrawing it as a fuller two-party exchange should therefore be treated as a purposeful explanatory enhancement, grounded in the slide text, rather than a faithful tracing of an existing diagram. Pages 17 and 21 are callouts rather than diagrams; they may be omitted as graphics and retained as styled text quotations/definitions.
