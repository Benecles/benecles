## A1. Reader brief

- **Already knows:** Aula 02's debate about constitutional courts, autonomy and legitimacy; what individual rights claims and a court order do. No prior knowledge of Colombian tutela or Brazilian ADPF is assumed.
- **Can do after reading:**
  1. From Duque Arcila's Bellavista claim and the institutional responses, identify evidence of a general violation, structural causes and a remedy involving more than the defendant.
  2. Give for **T-153/1998** and **ADPF 347/2015 and 2023**, separately, the court, year, holding, reasons, vote/dissent status and what followed.
  3. Compare who chooses means, who supervises, how affected prisoners can participate, and what would show that an ECI remedy worked.
  4. Answer the verbatim eixo with both cases, including a justified boundary on judicial action.
- **One sitting:** target 4,100–4,300 visible words, about 25–30 minutes. Baseline 3,770: keep at least that much substantive content; no unexplained word drop.

## A5. The thread

**Chosen:** the real **Bellavista tutela and its director's capacity/causation answer** (1,700 places, 4,969 occupants) as the diagnostic thread. Its limit is the prison director's inability to refuse court-ordered admissions. The two judgments are variations in what a constitutional court does once one prisoner's claim exposes the system: T-153's orders and ADPF 347's staged plans. The Brazilian case is a comparison, never a fictive Bellavista “appeal” or a transplant of Colombian tutela procedure.
