## A1. Operational learning core

**S0 outcome:** “Identificar as partes e classificar a pluralidade de sujeitos pelos dois eixos do litisconsórcio.” The later page must make the learner perform that outcome: identify who occupies the plaintiff and defendant positions in a concrete procedural situation; decide whether the multi-person arrangement is litisconsortium rather than merely multiple interested persons or separate cases; and classify it independently on the necessity and decision-uniformity axes. Locator: work/pipeline/processo-civil-i/course-map.json, aula-03 outcome and scope fields.

The lesson’s four operational tasks are:

1. Identify the parties to the claim and distinguish them from a third party who may be affected without being a party. Use Eid §1.1, assigned pp. 25–30 (PDF pp. 2–7), and CPC arts. 17–18 and 70–76 in 60-statute.txt.
2. Identify when there is a plurality of parties within the same proceeding, and name the configuration by plaintiff/defendant position. Use Didier, printed p. 510 (PDF pp. 508–509), Eid §1.2, printed pp. 30–33 (PDF pp. 7–10), and CPC art. 113. Test both decisions in CLOSE-06.
3. Classify necessity separately from the simple/unitary character of the decision. The first axis asks whether law or the nature of the relevant legal relationship requires participation by all; the second asks whether the merits must be decided uniformly for all members of that group. Use CPC arts. 114–116 and Didier, printed pp. 511–518 (PDF pp. 510–517), checked against Eid §1.2, printed pp. 33–48 (PDF pp. 10–25).
4. State the immediate procedural consequence of a necessary party’s absence at the level needed for recognition and issue spotting. Use CPC art. 115 and Didier, printed pp. 524–528 (PDF pp. 523–527). Do not expand into intervention procedure, correction mechanics, or a full effects taxonomy.

Teach a repeatable sequence: ask whether all must participate, then independently whether the merits must be uniform. Show four combinations only after defining both axes; keep examples source-bound. Locator: CPC arts. 113–116; Didier, printed pp. 511–518 (PDF pp. 510–517); Eid §1.2, assigned pp. 33–48 (PDF pp. 10–25).
