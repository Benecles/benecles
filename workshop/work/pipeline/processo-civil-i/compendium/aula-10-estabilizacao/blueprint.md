## A1. Reader brief

- **Already knows:** the response and preliminary measures have occurred; the parties have stated their positions; the general idea of preclusion and the procedural calendar. Course carry-forward: `aula-13.html` (post-answer preparation), `aula-03.html` (party positions), and `aula-07.html` (deadlines and preclusion). This is a reader assumption, not page copy.
- **Can do after reading** (tasks, not topics):
  1. Given the actual CPC provisions, locate the judgment paths in arts. 353, 355 and 356, then identify the work art. 357 performs when factual or evidentiary organization remains. (Tested once in close Q1.)
  2. Read the text of CPC art. 357 and match clauses I–V to pending procedure, facts and admitted evidence, burden, legal issues, and the hearing provision; identify the conditions stated in the article itself. (Tested once in close Q2.)
  3. Distinguish the parties’ proposal under § 2, the cooperative hearing under § 3, an art. 191 calendar, and the possible expert calendar under § 8. (Tested once in close Q3.)
  4. Explain what the common five-day request under § 1 can adjust and how the statutory stabilization point relates to the separate recourse path for an appealable chapter. (Tested once in close Q4.)
  5. State Lucon’s account of the prevalent view and Didier’s contrary distinction about revisiting an admissibility issue already decided. (Tested once in close Q5.)
- **Learning outcome, verbatim:** “Construir a organização da instrução e identificar o que a decisão de saneamento fixa para o restante do processo.”
- **One sitting:** about 4.4k words, 25–28 minutes at a steady legal-reading pace. Keep the page to one sitting; do not append a full evidence or judgment chapter.
