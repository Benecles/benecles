## A1. Reader brief

- **Already knows:** Aula 14’s general theory of proof, guiding principles, and argumentative/demonstrative models; Aula 08’s allegations of fact and specific impugnation in the contestation. These are direct prerequisites, not material to reteach.
- **Can do after reading** (tasks a question could ask, not topics):
  1. Given a real case record, isolate the factual assertions relevant to a claim or defense and distinguish the assertion from the past event it describes.
  2. Use CPC art. 374 to identify which factual assertions do not need proof and state what remains to be established.
  3. Evaluate a proposed method separately: whether it is legally and morally legitimate, whether its absence from the Code is dispositive, and whether the requested proof is necessary rather than useless or dilatory.
  4. Explain why a fact’s implausibility does not by itself answer whether a means of proof is admissible; answer both mapped exam questions.
- **Evidence of success:** the closing exercise is graded against four visible outputs: (a) name one exact factual assertion from the real autos; (b) state whether art. 374 removes it from proof, or mark that status as unstated; (c) identify the evidence source and apply arts. 369–370 to admissibility and necessity; (d) keep a conclusion about whether an allegation was made separate from a conclusion about whether the underlying event occurred. An answer that fills a gap in the record fails this criterion.
- **One sitting:** ~3.7k words, ~24–25 min. One lesson, no split: the outcome is a single two-part task—delimit the proof object, then assess a method—within S0’s 3,500-word estimate and the 2.5–4.5k range.
