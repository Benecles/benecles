## A1. Reader brief

- **Already knows:** how to identify the requirements of a procedural act (Aula 06); how procedural communication works (Aula 06-citação); and how to place a procedural event in time and recognize preclusion (Aula 07).
- **Can do after reading** (tasks a question could ask):
  1. Given an act and its governing rule, identify the departure from the prescribed model and explain why that departure does or does not support invalidity.
  2. Given a different form of performance, apply the statutory purpose test and distinguish preservation from a defect that still calls for correction or invalidation.
  3. Given when a party caused or first could raise a defect, apply the current CPC limits on who may request nullity and when.
  4. Given a curable defect or an invalidated act, select a supported correction and define the smallest set of later acts that must be repeated or rectified.
- **One sitting:** 3,800 words, 15–25 minutes.

The closing set has six items: adapted 2017 Q9; 2018 Q7; 2015 Q9; one paired item using both 2018 Q4 versions; 2025/2 P1 Q3 on MP intimation and prejudice; and one short act-dependency application based on arts. 281–283. Together they exercise each task above without restating a trap in multiple sections.

## A5. The thread

**Question for the whole piece:** when an act departs from its legal model, what supports keeping it, correcting it, or invalidating it, and how far does invalidity reach?

**Chosen:** CPC art. 277's limit phrase, **“lhe alcançar a finalidade”**, with the real art. 188/277 text as the recurring object. Variations are limited to the statutory conditions and the actual 2018 citation fact pattern; no invented law or fictional setting.

| Candidate | §01 | §02 | §03 | §04 | §05 | §06 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CPC art. 277: “lhe alcançar a finalidade”** | Direct rule for a form done another way. | Separates atipicity from consequence. | The purpose result does not erase arts. 276/278, and Calmon's 2005 treatment of timing is distinct from current art. 278. | Contrasts preservation with a defect that needs a correction route. | If an act is invalidated instead, arts. 281–283 limit what follows. | Supplies the purpose question; art. 239 adds the specific citation cure. | **Chosen:** one real statutory clause carries the core question through all sections, with the local statute controlling each limit. |
| CPC art. 239, § 1º: appearance cures citation absence/nullity | Too specific for general form. | A citation example only. | Starts a response period, but does not state art. 278's objection rule. | A special cure, not the general correction route. | Does not define propagation. | Strong carrier for the exam case. | Local carrier for §06, not the lesson thread. |
| CPC art. 282: “atos atingidos” and prejudice | Consequences, not the initial form test. | Helps distinguish defect from result. | Does not locate first opportunity. | Supports targeted correction. | Central to scope and prejudice. | Helps test whether repetition is needed. | Local carrier for §05; cannot carry the form question through the whole page. |

The thread is tested against each section, not pasted as a slogan. The sections begin with the meaning of “finalidade”, then distinguish defect from consequence, locate the objection in time, compare preservation with correction, delimit the consequences of invalidation, and finally apply the sequence to the citation record.
