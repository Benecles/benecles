# SLOP-2 source notes E

Scope: transferable guidance for Portuguese-language legal teaching materials. These projects are editorial heuristics, not validated authorship tests. Favor clarity, source fidelity, and the document's teaching/legal register over detector scores.

- **[se-uhd/ai-slop-skill](https://github.com/se-uhd/ai-slop-skill)** — Separates general prose rules from scientific/LaTeX conventions and optional Simplified Technical English; quotations and literal strings are preserved, and where simplification loses precision the author decides. **Adopt** layered rules and “preserve meaning before simplification”; **adapt** STE's plain-language principles (one topic per paragraph, active voice, verbs over nominalizations) for students while retaining legal terms and statutory wording; **reject** its dependency on a live English trope catalog and blanket sentence-length targets as a Portuguese legal standard. Useful architecture, but not proof that a flagged trope indicates AI.
- **[hannsxpeter/humanizer](https://github.com/hannsxpeter/humanizer/blob/main/SKILL.md)** — Argues that repetitive rhythm and uniform sentence shapes matter more than word substitution; calls for preserving voice and real details and warns against a new “humanizer dialect.” **Adopt** variety where natural and conservative, meaning-preserving edits; **adapt** by varying explanation, example, and sentence rhythm only when it aids comprehension; **reject** any detector-evasion framing or substitutions made solely to avoid a word list. A useful explicit safeguard: correctness, formal vocabulary, and one dash are not evidence of AI.
- **[eric-sabe/slop-lint](https://github.com/eric-sabe/slop-lint)** — Its README treats em dashes as a hard failure but other vocabulary and cliché hits only as review warnings, explicitly because such words also occur in good human prose. **Adopt** the warning-not-ban principle and allow-listing legitimate terminology; **adapt** any lexicon to Brazilian Portuguese and the course corpus; **reject** its categorical em-dash failure as a style gate. Legal argumentation may need punctuation and terms that a generic lint rule mistakes for a tell.
- **[conorbronsdon/avoid-ai-writing: `patterns.md`](https://github.com/conorbronsdon/avoid-ai-writing/blob/main/references/patterns.md) and [tolerance matrix](https://github.com/conorbronsdon/avoid-ai-writing/blob/main/references/patterns.md#tolerance-matrix)** — Its 74-pattern catalog scopes findings by context; the matrix allows technical/documentation registers to retain lists, cautious hedging, and legitimate technical vocabulary. It distinguishes clarity edits from authorship signals and advises context-sensitive judgment. **Adopt** genre- and audience-specific tolerance plus pattern clusters over isolated words; **adapt** the profiles to legal teaching (statutory quotation, doctrinal caveat, case brief, classroom instruction); **reject** importing English terms, thresholds, or profiles unchanged. Treat the matrix as an editorial aid, not a Portuguese detector calibration.
- **[keez97/humanizer](https://github.com/keez97/humanizer/blob/main/SKILL.md)** — Its strongest safeguards say never invent facts, dates, numbers, citations, or first-person experience, and that analytical writing must preserve uncertainty labels. It also mandates deliberate imperfection in long-form prose, including an unclear sentence, unresolved tangent, or uncertain first-person recommendation. **Adopt** the anti-fabrication and uncertainty-preservation safeguards; **adapt** genuine teacher voice only when present and supported; **reject outright** the planted-imperfection mandate. Do not introduce awkward syntax, tangents, fabricated hesitation, fake anecdotes, or errors to simulate humanity—these harm legal accuracy and student comprehension. Preserve authentic imperfections already in a quoted source, without silently correcting or “humanizing” them.
- **[Bouchard, “Anti-Slop AI Writing Guide” (full prompt template)](https://github.com/louisfb01/ai-engineering-cheatsheets/blob/main/Anti_Slop_AI_Writing_Guide.md)** — Organizes writing around purpose, audience/outcomes, a supplied structure and evidence base; explicitly bars invented facts and asks for source verification, then a focused human editorial pass. **Adopt** audience/outcome-first planning, a defined teaching job per paragraph, evidence-led drafting, and final accuracy/voice review; **adapt** its structure so the legal question, governing source, reasoning, and learning objective are explicit; **reject** the fixed ban on em dashes, banned-word lists, “zero” pattern targets, and its promotional/content-marketing voice as universal requirements. Its prompt is a workflow template, not evidence that the recommended bans improve legal pedagogy.

## Cross-source decision

Use a Portuguese-native, legal-education checklist: (1) every legal claim is traceable to an authoritative source and retains its scope, date, and uncertainty; (2) quotations and citations remain intact; (3) explain doctrine in direct language without replacing terms of art; (4) remove empty previews, recap-only conclusions, inflated significance, and repeated templates when they impede learning; (5) preserve intentional formality, caveats, and useful repetition. A pattern is a reason to inspect a passage, never a reason by itself to infer authorship.

**Planted imperfections:** expressly reject adding grammatical errors, deliberate obscurity, forced first-person opinions, unresolved tangents, or fabricated “human” details. The keez97 requirement to force at least one such imperfection is incompatible with legal teaching and with the same source's no-fabrication guard. Human voice should come from the instructor's actual choices, examples, and explanations, not synthetic roughness.
