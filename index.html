01 What we sell

The course is the output. The compiler is the product.

Ordenações Filipinas, seven law courses for the 2026/2 semester at UFRGS, is the first thing the compiler built. What we offer is the run itself: the same stations, standards and checks, pointed at a new syllabus.

In
  • The syllabus, in its own order
  • The books, slide decks, decisions and statutes it assigns
  • Past exams and exercise lists
Out
  • One lesson per syllabus topic, 15 to 25 minutes each
  • Figures drawn as instruments: a deadline on the real calendar, a statute as a balance
  • Every quotation found in its source, by page
  • Each exam trap placed once and tested once
  • A static site that reads offline and needs no server
  • The ledger of which chapter fed which lesson, and why

02 The line

Ten stations, a gate after each.

The order is fixed: understand the course, prepare the material, then write. No station starts until the one before it passes its check. Below, one real lesson goes down the line: Processo Civil I, Aula 06, on citação and intimação, the formal notices that bring a party into a lawsuit.

A different model judges the blueprint the way a thesis committee judges a proposal. It reads the plan, the course map, the workbench index and the exam questions, never the chapters, and answers APPROVED or REVISE with at most five numbered points. A second REVISE goes to Claude. 30 reviews.

The prose backtest18× the human rate

The backtest found our own habit. Pages used “não basta”, “não substitui” and their kin at 2.43 per thousand words, about eighteen times the rate in human legal doctrine. It became a rule, with its limit set where no more than one human text in ten would trip it. Method and numbers.

Every fix adds a bug row with the check that now catches it. Every lesson learned at a gate becomes a numbered rule that each station in flight applies before its next step.

04 Built with Claude

Claude runs the house.

The work is done by AI agents with defined jobs, coordinated in the open on GitHub. Benecles sets the direction; Claude turns it into briefs and holds the standard; Codex supplies the volume; scripts decide what passes.

Direction

Benecles

Chooses the courses and makes the calls no agent should: what to build, what to cut, what the house stands for.

Editor and designer

Claude

Writes every brief: what the thing is, how it must look, what it must never do. Owns the writing standard and the design system, draws figures, rebuilds reference lessons by hand and gates each station. Anthropic, through Claude Code.

The floor

Codex

An orchestrator splits each brief into units of six or fewer and runs up to sixteen workers at once: Sol for the course map and the panel, Luna for everything else. OpenAI.

The gate

Scripts

Every station has a check that can fail, and nothing merges red. The checks are in the repository, next to the pages they judge.
